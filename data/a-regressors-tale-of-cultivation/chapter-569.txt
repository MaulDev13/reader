"In the name of this Immortal, I prophesize. All pursuers in this galaxy will, at minimum, lose their targets at least once."

When using prophecy, there is always a limit to its scope.

If the range of the prophecy is too broad, it will shatter before it can affect the entire scope, or it may become invalid.

When that happens, the star of prophecy collapses, and the one who uttered the prophecy suffers immense backlash.

Thus, when using a prophecy on a particular target, it's crucial to define the target precisely and attach specific conditions to increase the success rate.

However, there are instances when exceptions occur.

When a single being possesses an overwhelming influence and presence that surpasses an entire multitude.

'Glass Peacock...their presence and authority exceeds that of every living being in this galaxy combined.'

In such cases, it is far more effective to direct the prophecy not at the individual but at the fate tied to the entire galaxy.

Rather than making a prophecy involving a single Glass Peacock, it is far less likely to fail to make a prophecy that loosely applies to the galaxy where Glass Peacock currently resides.

'When prophesying about a being with greater presence than myself, the prophecy must target not the being but the places or objects associated with them.'

This is one of the pieces of wisdom imparted by Blood Yin.

Bearing the pain, I create a prophetic star and hand it to Golden Shaking Bird. She accepts it, stores it within her body, and then makes her own prophecy, sealing the stars within her so that no other being can interfere.

"Now! Go!"

It seems as though Yeo Hwi is screaming something, but Glass Peacock, riding the interstellar wind, moves so fast that even the mentally speech of someone at the Sacred Vessel stage like Yeo Hwi is barely audible.

'Why, though?'

Do you know why Golden Shaking Bird tore off a piece of her Immortal Body to give to him? : :

Manipulating fate requires a price.

To guide fate as one desires, one must possess the corresponding amount of fortune (blessing).

Since from True Immortals onwards, one's fate is their talent, the fortune one is born with sets the basic limit for how many prophecies they can utter.

While advancing one's realm may allow them to exceed these basic limits, every True Immortal has an inherent cap on their prophetic ability.

Upon reaching the Earth Axis stage, one learns that the concept of 'extremity' signifies a state of 'being without blessings'.

Every True Immortal who overuses prophecy beyond their limits eventually falls into extremity, rendering them incapable of affecting any fate.

'But there's a way to replenish it.'
